TL;DRTeleoperation pays for every demonstration, and every new object instance pays again. Real2Sem2Real (R2Sem2R) turns one human RGB video into thousands of robot demonstrations across the object instances of a category, with no depth sensor and no teleoperation. It tracks the demonstrated objects, generates digital cousins of them from photographs, aligns the cousins to the demonstration by their semantics, and replays the demonstration for every cousin pairing in simulation.

01 Method

From one human video to thousands of robot demonstrations

Step through the pipeline. Most steps are live 3D: drag to orbit, or use the arrow keys to move between steps.

  1. One human RGB video of the task and a phone scan of the objects.
  2. Each object becomes a Gaussian splat with DINOv3 features, tracked through the video from RGB alone.
  3. The human grasp is retargeted to a parallel-jaw gripper on the demonstrated mug.
  4. Cousin mugs are aligned to it by semantic ICP, and the grasp carries over.
  5. Coffee machines are aligned on the tray with task-weighted ICP.
  6. Thousands of episodes are generated in IsaacSim and MuJoCo.
  7. A policy trained on them runs on a real xArm7 and Franka Panda.

02 Tracking

Tracking objects from RGB alone, more accurately than the alternatives

Each tracker's pose is rendered over the human video, frame by frame. On the seven objects of our four tasks, our tracker has a mean mask IoU of 0.913, against 0.681 for WARPED, 0.629 for FoundationPose with monocular metric depth and 0.415 for RSRD.

05 Physics

A generated episode, replayed live in MuJoCo

MuJoCo, compiled to WebAssembly, re-simulates the scene one of our generated episodes was made in, with contact. Drag to orbit.

How the replay works, and what the arrows show

The robot replays the episode's recorded joint targets at 10 Hz while the mug, the table and the coffee machine are simulated with contact; as in our data generation, a soft weld holds the mug once the finger pads close on the handle and releases it on the tray. The generator keeps only episodes whose mug comes to rest on the tray, and each of the six object pairs ends on the tray when replayed here. The arrows show the forces on the mug, longer for larger forces: from the fingers in red, the table in blue and the coffee machine in orange. While the hand carries the mug, the red arrows at the fingertips show the force the weld applies to hold it. The panel gives the totals; resting on the table or the tray, or carried at a steady pace, they add up to the mug's weight, 2.9 N.

06 Limitations

What it needs, and what it does not handle yet

A photo per cousin

Every cousin needs at least one photograph. Generating a category from text alone is future work.

A known supporting surface

Monocular depth is up to scale, which we resolve per scene from the recovered ground plane, so the demonstration must take place on a known supporting surface.

A clean grasp in the video

Grasp recovery needs a moment where the hand holds the object rigidly. Sliding or tool use without a grasp falls outside its scope.

Rigid objects only

The tracker estimates one rigid pose per object, so soft and deformable objects are not supported. Articulated objects such as drawers, doors and cabinets are the natural next step.
