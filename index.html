Teleoperation pays for every demonstration, and every new object instance pays again. Real2Sem2Real (R2Sem2R) turns one human RGB video into thousands of robot demonstrations across the object instances of a category, with no depth sensor and no teleoperation. It tracks the demonstrated objects, generates digital cousins of them from photographs, aligns the cousins to the demonstration by their semantics, and replays the demonstration for every cousin pairing in simulation.

  • Digital cousins generated from one or a few photographs
  • Semantic alignment of whole objects and of functional regions such as a coffee machine's tray

01

Why one human video?

A person can demonstrate a task with a phone camera in seconds. Turning that video into robot training data has meant either a depth sensor, overlaying a robot onto 2D video, or generating data only for the exact objects that were demonstrated. A policy trained on one mug and one coffee machine does not transfer to the next mug.

Existing real-to-sim pipelines also break at three recurring points: the object track drifts, the recovered grasp collides with the object, and the reconstructed meshes look nothing like the real scene.

02

How does R2Sem2R work?

Method overview. From a single human demonstration with no teleoperation, we recover the mug's trajectory, generate a 16 × 9 library of cousin mugs and coffee machines, and render thousands of robot demonstrations in simulation. The resulting policy succeeds on in- and out-of-distribution objects.

Step 1
Track

An RGB-only 4D tracker built on DINO-distilled Gaussian splats follows each object's pose through the human video.

Step 2
Generate cousins

SAM3 and SAM3D turn one or a few photographs and a text prompt into a cousin splat and mesh. The photos can come from the real world, an online store, or an image generator.

Step 3
Align by semantics

DINOv3 features distilled into every splat align each cousin to the demonstrated object, by the whole object for a mug and by the functional region, such as the tray, for a coffee machine.

Step 4
Generate and train

The demonstration is replayed for every cousin pairing in IsaacSim, with kinematic replay, or MuJoCo, with contact physics, and a VLA policy is trained on the result.

Every later stage depends on the object track, so we compare against the two closest alternatives on the seven objects of our four tasks. Every method gets the same object model, and every method is scored by per-frame mask IoU against the same SAM2 masks.

How well does it work?

We fine-tune the π0.5 vision-language-action model on three kinds of data and evaluate on real robots: teleoperation, 150 real demonstrations collected across several objects; R2R2R, 2,000 generated episodes of the single demonstrated object pair, following Real2Render2Real; and R2Sem2R, 2,000 generated episodes across digital cousins.

Limitations

A photo per cousin

Every cousin needs at least one photograph. Generating a category from text alone is future work.

A known supporting surface

Monocular depth is up to scale, which we resolve per scene from the recovered ground plane, so the demonstration must take place on a known supporting surface.

A clean grasp in the video

Grasp recovery needs a moment where the hand holds the object rigidly. Sliding or tool use without a grasp falls outside its scope.

Rigid objects only

Articulated objects such as drawers, doors and cabinets are the natural next step.

08

Q&A

Why use RGB video only?

A phone always has a camera, but not always a depth camera. Working from RGB alone means any phone video of a person doing the task can become robot data.

What is a digital cousin?

A 3D model of another object of the same category, such as a different mug, generated from one or a few photographs and a text prompt. SAM3 segments the object and SAM3D generates a splat and a textured mesh; ten cousins take about seven minutes on one GPU. The photos can be taken in the real world, found in an online store, or generated by an image model.

How is this different from Real2Render2Real?

R2R2R augments the poses of the objects that were demonstrated. R2Sem2R adds semantic augmentation: the same demonstration is transferred to new object instances. Trained on the same number of episodes, the cousins raise mug placement on the xArm7 from 36% to 90%.

Why align objects by semantics rather than shape?

Cousins differ in shape, so lining up their geometry does not line up the parts that matter. DINOv3 features distilled into each splat let us match corresponding parts. For placement objects, the alignment is weighted toward the functional region, such as a coffee machine's tray, rather than the whole object.

Which simulators do you use?

Both IsaacSim, with kinematic replay rendered in parallel on the GPU, and MuJoCo, which simulates contact physics and keeps an episode only if the object comes to rest at its goal.
